# Evaluation Answers

This page provides the reference answers to the 30 questions used to evaluate PRISM.


## Reference Answers
1 To assess the safety and efficacy of hepatocyte-like cell (HLC) infusion therapy derived from human embryonic stem cells as bridging therapy for neonatal-onset urea cycle disorders (UCD).

2 Five neonates received HLC infusions.

3 A target weight of over 6 kg was considered necessary for safer liver transplantation.

4 SCID-OTCD mice (SCID mice models of UCD) were used.

5 Approximately 60% of the HLC population is expected to differentiate into functional hepatocytes.

6 The ESC cell line SEES2 was used to produce the master cell bank.

7 Because it has undergone ex-vivo exposure with mouse embryonic fibroblasts.

8 All five neonates exceeded the target weight of 6 kg while maintaining metabolic stability and successfully bridging to transplantation; post-transplantation follow-up revealed stable growth, metabolic control, and no neurological complications.

9 Yes; the study was conducted with prior approval from the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) as a first-in-human trial.

10 They were thawed, washed, and re-suspended in saline with 5% human albumin at a density of 1 × 10^7 cells/mL.

11 A double-lumen catheter (18G, Medicut LCV-UK; Tyco Healthcare, NJ, USA) was inserted into the left portal vein via the umbilical vein.

12 The proximal end was used for assessing portal pressure, while the distal end was used for HLC infusion.

13 1 mL was administered over 1–5 min, followed by a 2 to 10-min dosing pause.

14 The lateral segment of the liver was chosen and HLCs were infused in two doses of 2.5 × 10^7 cells/kg each, with the second dose given 2–3 days after the first; before the second dose portal pressure had to be within 1.25 times the value before the first dose and portal blood flow velocity at least 0.75 times higher.

15 Administration was temporarily suspended if portal venous blood flow velocity decreased by 50% or greater from pre-administration; administration was resumed when the decrease was 25% or less.

16 Portal venography was performed to check for patency of the ductus venosus, and if the ductus arantius was open, it was closed before injection.

17 The first HLC infusion was performed under sedation and ventilator control, and the second and subsequent infusion was performed without ventilator control in the intensive care unit using an indwelling catheter.

18 Tacrolimus and prednisolone were used as immunosuppressants.

19 A single dose of 5.5 × 10^7 cells/kg was injected via the portal vein into male and female nude rats and toxicity was studied for 12 weeks; no changes suggestive of toxicity were observed.

20 HLCs had no tumorigenic potential at 16 weeks after a single dose of 1 × 10^7 cells was subcutaneously injected into nude mice whereas HeLa S3 cells formed tumors in all cases; injection of undifferentiated ESCs at ratios of 1%, 10%, or 100% to the number of HLCs all formed teratomas.

21 Thrombi/emboli were revealed in two cases (P3 and P4).

22 The embolus was accompanied by chondrogenesis.

23 No evidence of differentiated cells other than hepatocytes and chondrocytes was detected, and no clear teratoma formation was observed.

24 No ischemic changes were noted in any of the cases.

25 The contrast-enhanced CT scan at three months post-hepatocyte transplantation showed luminal narrowing indicative of an embolism.

26 No, examination of the explanted liver tissue revealed no detectable HLCs.

27 The authors propose that HLCs serve a temporary functional role as a bridge to transplantation and did not persist long-term nor undergo significant proliferation.

28 Lymphocytic infiltration suggests that immune-mediated rejection may have contributed to the disappearance of HLCs.

29 Ammonia levels remained stable for a median duration of 1 year and 5 months, with a range of 136–882 days.

30 Formally approved on April 28, 2018, reference number: C29007.
